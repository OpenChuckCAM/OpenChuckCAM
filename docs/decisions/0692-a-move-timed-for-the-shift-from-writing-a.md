## Context

Path assembly times every fed move so that the F word the post writes stays within the limit gate 1 applies to the written block (decisions 0034, 0050 and 0076). Two rules decide that limit's side:

1. **Path assembly** (`openchuck-path`, `assemble.rs`, `Builder::push`) chooses the line limit from the move's exact (unrounded) changes: the linear limit if X or Y changes by a written unit (0.001 mm) or more, the lower of the linear and Z limits if X or Y changes by less, and Z's limit if neither changes (decision 0050 item 6). The line is timed for its length plus the written allowance of 0.002 mm.
2. **Gate 1** (`openchuck-gcode`, `check.rs`, `FeedLimits::for_move`) holds the F word to the linear limit if the written X or Y changes, else to Z's if the written Z changes.

Since issue #780 (lane RUNOUT-TIP, on `main` since 11 Nov 2026 and in 0.55.0), the post (`openchuck-post`, `convert.rs`, `target_of`) rounds A to its written decimals first and works X, Y and Z out at that A, so the controller puts the tool on the model point at the A it receives. Rounding A by up to half a unit (0.0005 degree, 8.7e-6 radian) turns each end point by up to that angle about the rotary axis: it moves by up to `r` times 8.7e-6 mm, `r` being its distance from the axis. Path assembly's rule 1 did not allow for that movement, so a change of Y "by a written unit or more" could still be written as no change at all.

**The reporter's move** (instrumented by the owner's analysis session; verified by this lane): a simultaneous-finishing move of the 3.175 mm ball, 21.85 mm from the axis, with exact changes `dx` 0.000018, `dy` 0.001096 and `dz` 0.041942 mm and `dA` 0.005372 degree. `dy` is over 0.001 mm, so the move was timed for the linear limit (1200). Each end moved up to 0.00019 mm when A was written, and both ends' Y were written `-4.871`; the block `Z21.299 A32328.348` (after `Y-4.871 Z21.257 A32328.342`) changes only Z and A, so gate 1 held it to Z's limit. Under the linear limit the move's time was set by the per-axis term `dz / z` with the exact `dz`; the written `dz` is 0.042, so the F word was 0.042 / 0.041942 = 1.0014 times Z's limit: `F650.9` on the reporter's machine copy (Z 650), `F600.8` on the built-in Z1 (Z 600). A second move of the same job failed the same way (`F652.2`, `dy` 0.001128 mm). Changing the feed or the limit does not help: the overshoot follows Z's limit. It is not a machine-safety defect (gate 1 refuses the file, and the controller caps Z's rate), but no user workaround exists.

## Decision

1. **The shift.** For a fed move, path assembly computes `s = (r0 + r1 + 0.002) * 0.0005 degree in radians`, with `r0` and `r1` the two ends' distances from the rotary axis (the 0.002 allows for the coordinates across the axis written to three decimals, as decision 0076 item 2 does for `r`). It bounds how much writing A can change the move between its ends, in any direction across the axis. It is 0.00038 mm at `r` 22 mm and 0.00079 mm at 45 mm (the Z1's recommended safe Z). Rapids are unchanged: they have no F word.
2. **The line limit (amends decision 0050 item 6).** The band below which a change of X or Y may be written either way is `0.001 + s`, not 0.001: a fed move that changes X or Y by less than that is timed for the lower of the linear and Z limits. A move that changes X or Y by `0.001 + s` or more is written with that change at any A rounding, so gate 1 holds it to the linear limit, as the move is timed. Both X and Y use the band. On an X-rotary machine only Y and Z move when A is rounded, but the same band for both keeps the rule frame-free (a Y-rotary machine moves X).
3. **The written allowance (amends decision 0076 item 2).** The line of a fed move is timed for its length plus `0.002 + s`, not 0.002. The 0.002 covers rounding each of three coordinates at two ends (at most `2 * sqrt(3) * 0.0005` = 0.00173 mm); writing A adds at most `s`. Without it, a move held to the line's limit could be written up to `s` longer than it is timed for, so above about 15 mm from the axis an unlucky rounding of all four values could still give an F word a fraction over the limit.
4. **What does not change.** One IR for every post (decision 0007 item 3, GC-10): the rule is in path assembly and gives the same times for the LinuxCNC and Z1 posts. The post does not clamp the F word (rejected in decision 0076, alternative 1), and gate 1 is not loosened. The post's A rounding (issue #780) stays: it keeps the tool tip on the model within the linear rounding budget. `MaxFeeds`, the machine profiles and every public item are unchanged.

## Options not chosen

- **(a) Give the per-axis terms the written allowance as well** (`dx` and `dy` over the linear limit and `dz` over Z's limit timed for the change as written). It would also have fixed the reporter's two moves: the written `dz` of 0.042 timed against 600 gives exactly 600. It leaves the cause in place: a move timed for the linear limit whose X and Y are written unchanged is still held to Z's limit, and only the per-axis term saves it, which holds only while the line limit's term does not set the time. It also lengthens every steep fed move whose Z term sets its time (by `0.001 / dz`), which changes F words in programs that pass today. The per-axis terms concern the controller's own per-axis caps, which gate 1 does not check; whether they should carry the allowance is recorded as OI-2216.
- **Clamp the F word in the post.** Rejected in decision 0076 (alternative 1): the post would no longer keep the IR's time (GC-09), and gate 2 compares that time.
- **Loosen gate 1** (a tolerance on the F word). The written word would then be over the machine's limit as the controller reads it.
- **Undo the post's A rounding (issue #780).** It would bring back the radius-dependent tip error that #780 fixed.

## Consequences

- A fed move that changes X or Y by between 0.001 and `0.001 + s` mm is now timed for the lower of the linear and Z limits: on the Z1 (linear 1200, Z 600) it takes up to twice as long where the line limit sets its time. Such moves are rare: they change X or Y by a micron or so.
- A fed move whose time the line limit sets is timed `s` longer: at most 0.00079 mm at 45 mm from the axis, so its F word drops by at most `s` over the move's length.
- Golden files: see *Verification*.
- **Library family:** `openchuck-path` changes the time of some fed moves (output), with no public API change; its `CHANGELOG.md` records the fix. The workspace moves to 0.64.0 at release, as the coordinator assigned in merge order (an output change of a library crate bumps the workspace minor, decision 0048).
- **Hotfix:** the same rule is in 0.55.0 (the post's A rounding came with #780 on 11 Nov 2026, before the 0.55.0 release commit `57b8ac1` of 22 Nov 2026). The lane prepares the backport as 0.55.1 on `release/0.55.x`; tagging and publishing are the owner's.

## Amendment 1: fed moves with no exact change of X or Y (accepted 26 Nov 2026, relayed; issue #943)

**Context.** CodeRabbit's second review of pull request #939 found a case the accepted decision does not reach. A fed move that turns A with the tip exactly fixed (no change of X, Y or Z in the machine's frame) has no line, so path assembly held it to A's own limit alone, as decision 0076 item 2 says ("A move with no XYZ motion is held to A's own limit, as before"). Since issue #780 the post works XYZ out at A written to three decimals, so the two ends are turned by different amounts and can be written with a change of X or Y: at 100 mm from the axis, A from 0.0004 to 0.0006 degree is written `Y0.001 ... A0.000` then `Y-0.001 ... A0.001`. Gate 1 then holds the block's F word, the written line's speed (LinuxCNC) or the larger of it and A's counted travel (the Z1), to the linear limit; timed for A alone it was about 6000 against 1000, and gate 1 refuses the file. Found in no golden file and not in the reporter's job of issue #929: it needs a turn of a few thousandths of a degree. 0.55.0 has it too.

**Decision.**

1. **A fed move that turns A with no exact change of X, Y or Z is timed for the written line as well** (amends decision 0076 item 2's "A move with no XYZ motion is held to A's own limit, as before", for fed moves): for a line of length 0 written as up to `0.002 + s` mm long, over the lower of the linear and Z limits (decision 0692 item 2 already holds such a move to that lower limit). Its time is the larger of that and A's own term, as for every other fed move. A rapid is unchanged.
2. **Every fed move whose change of X and Y is below `0.001 + s`, none included, is timed for the lower of the linear and Z limits, whatever A does** (completes item 2 of the decision above, and amends decision 0050 item 6's "A move with no X or Y keeps Z's limit" for fed moves). Item 2 above already covers a change of zero in its words, but as built the code kept Z's limit for a move with no exact change of X or Y. Such a move can be written with a change of Y: when A turns, its two ends are turned by different amounts; when it does not, at an A not on three decimals both ends are turned by the same angle, but ends at different heights move by slightly different amounts (CodeRabbit's first and third comments on pull request #939; the first, for a move that turns A, was fixed in that pull request). Where Z is no faster than the linear axes (the Z1 and every built-in profile) the lower limit is Z's, so nothing changes; on a machine whose Z is faster, such fed moves are timed for the linear limit.
3. Nothing else changes: one IR for every post, no clamp in the post, gate 1 unchanged.

**Consequences.** The shortest time of such a move is `60 * (0.002 + s) / min(linear, Z)` seconds: 0.00022 s on the Z1 at 45 mm from the axis. A's own term is longer than that for any turn above about 0.006 degree on the Z1, so only turns of a few thousandths of a degree take longer. Golden files: see *Verification*.

**Library family.** `openchuck-path` changes output, with no public API change; the workspace moves to 0.66.0, as the coordinator assigned in merge order (decision 0048).

**Options not chosen.** Leave it as it is (gate 1 refuses the file, the safe direction, but the user cannot work round it). Write such a turn without the A rounding in the post (two IRs, against GC-10, or a post rule that undoes issue #780 for some moves).

## Amendment 2: a fed turn of A with the tip fixed on a controller that counts A (accepted 26 Nov 2026, relayed; issue #948)

**Context.** CodeRabbit's review of pull request #945 found the last uncovered case. On a controller that counts A's travel round the part in its F word (the Makera Z1, `rotary_in_feed = "makera"`), a fed turn of A with the tool tip fixed was held to A's own limit (decision 0076 item 2) and, after amendment 1, to the written line; it was not timed for A's counted travel. If writing A gives the block a change of X, Y or Z, the Z1 counts A's travel with it, `max(L, dA * (2 pi r + 30) / 360)`, and gate 1 holds that F word to the linear limit. At 100 mm from the axis, A from 0.0004 to 0.0106 degree gave about `F3222` against 1000, and gate 1 refused the file. A's own limit keeps it within only while `2 pi r + 30` is at most about 240 mm (r up to 33 mm on the Z1).

**The family, reviewed.** The lane reviewed every way the post's A rounding can make the written block differ from what assembly timed, for fed moves (a rapid has no F word that gate 1 checks). Gate 1's limit follows the written block: the linear limit if X or Y changes, Z's if only Z, A's if only A.

| Written change | Timed for it after decision 0692 and amendment 1 | After amendment 2 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| X or Y | the line plus `0.002 + s` over a limit no higher than the linear limit | unchanged |
| Z only | the band rule gives a limit no higher than Z's | unchanged |
| A only | `(dA + 0.001)` over A's limit (decision 0647) | unchanged |
| X, Y or Z on a fixed-tip turn, on a counting controller | the written line only: **not** A's counted travel (this amendment's case) | the written line **and** A's counted travel |
| nothing | no F word is checked | unchanged |

**Decision.**

1. **On a controller that counts A in its F word, a fed turn of A with no exact change of X, Y or Z is timed for A's counted travel as well, where writing A moves an end** (amends decision 0076 item 2's "A move with no XYZ motion is held to A's own limit, as before", for such turns): where either end's A is not on a written unit (three decimals), the post works that end's XYZ out at a different angle and can write a change of X, Y or Z, so the turn's line term is the larger of the written line (`0.002 + s`) and `(dA + 0.001) * (2 pi max(r + 0.001, 1) + 30) / 360`, over the lower of the linear and Z limits. A turn whose two ends are both on a written unit is written with no change of XYZ (the post's A is the exact A), so it keeps A's own limit, as decision 0076 item 2 says.
2. Nothing else changes: rapids, one IR for every post, no clamp in the post, gate 1 unchanged.

**Consequences.** Only fed A turns about a fixed tip at an angle not on a written unit, on a counting controller, take longer: at 100 mm from the axis on the Z1 they are held to about 600 mm/min of counted travel. Turns between angles on a written unit, such as the 90-degree index turns of the `first_cut` reference job's roughing, are unchanged. Golden files: none changes (see *Verification*). Library family: `openchuck-path` changes output, with no public API change; the workspace moves to 0.67.0, as the coordinator assigned in merge order (decision 0048).

**Options not chosen.**

- **A: count A's travel on every fed A-only turn.** It also covers turns between angles on a written unit, which are written with no change of XYZ, and so reverses decision 0076 item 2 where nothing needs it. Measured: it slows the `first_cut` reference job's 90-degree roughing turns from `F865.3` to `F599.9` on the Z1 (`F1799.6` to `F1247.6` on LinuxCNC; one IR), adds about 38 s to its roughing (3779 s to 3817 s), and fails decision 0076's own test.
- **B′: the post writes no change of XYZ on a block whose exact tip is fixed.** A writing rule in the post that undoes issue #780 for those blocks and brings back a tip error of up to `r` times 0.0005 degree there.
- **Leave it.** Gate 1 refuses such a file (the safe direction), but the user cannot work round it.

**Also found: posts with fewer decimals** (OI-2221). Every written allowance in path assembly assumes positions and A written to three decimals. The two built-in posts write three; a post the user writes with fewer could round further than the allowances, and gate 1 would then refuse the file. Not changed here.
